Garage Door Opener Troubleshooting

Many “dead opener” calls are dirty photo eyes, a dead remote battery, or a door that is too heavy because a spring failed. Work through these checks before you buy a new unit.

Checks you can do without tools

If the motor runs but the door barely moves, disconnect the opener with the red release handle. Lift the door by hand. If it feels like a sack of concrete, the springs failed. The opener is doing a job it was not built for. Stop and see garage door won't open.

If the door goes down a few inches and comes back up, look at the two photo-eye sensors near the floor. The LEDs should be steady. Wipe the lenses. Make sure nothing in the track is blocking the beam. Sun glare on west-facing Orange County garages fools sensors in the late afternoon. Shade the eye or slide it a half inch.

Remotes: replace the battery first. If the wall button still works, the motor is fine. If nothing works, check that the opener has power and the lock switch on the wall console is not on.

A grinding opener with a door that still lifts usually means a worn gear kit inside a chain-drive head. That is a parts job, not a new machine, if the rail and motor are sound. Same-day repair: opener repair in Orange County and door won't close.

  • Motor runs, door heavy: check springs first
  • Door reverses at the floor: clean and aim photo eyes
  • Remote dead, wall button works: battery or learn button
  • Click or grind from the head: gear kit, not always a new opener
